# Data card and ethical reflection — Knotweed Checker

**Data.** 832 photos from Dutch iNaturalist users, not taken by us: 302 knotweed (*Reynoutria*) and 530 other plants: bindweed, *Persicaria* and *Bistorta* (some native, some garden plants), *Fallopia* (black-bindweed, Russian vine), bramble, nettle and ivy. They are mostly close-ups taken by nature enthusiasts in summer and autumn, often of large, mature plants. Licences are CC0 or CC-BY(-NC/-SA); every photo's author, licence and link is in `dataset/sources.csv`. Labels come from the community-confirmed species, not from our search term. iNaturalist's search mixed knotweed and bindweed, which put about 70 photos in the wrong class until we caught it. We also removed 26 photos of a rust fungus on bramble leaves. The "not knotweed" class covers only these few plant groups, not the whole Dutch flora.

**How we built and tested the model.** Photos were scraped with `scrape_inaturalist.py`: only research-grade observations (identity confirmed by the community) from the Netherlands. In Roboflow we made a classification project where the folder name is the label. We split by plant, not by photo: the 302 knotweed photos come from only 185 plants, so all photos of one plant go in the same split (587/172/73 train/valid/test). Otherwise the model could be tested on a plant it already saw. Training photos were doubled with flips, small rotations and brightness changes (`train_roboflow.py`). Model: Roboflow's ViT classifier (`vit-base-patch16-224-in21k`), version `knotweed-nl/1`. Test (`evaluate.py`, same 70% rule as the app): 95% of the 73 test photos right, 24 of 26 knotweed found, 2 of 47 other plants wrongly flagged. Accuracy alone flatters it: always answering "not knotweed" would already score 64%.

**Limitations and failure modes.** The model has rarely seen young red shoots in spring, bare brown winter stems, or knotweed hidden among other plants, so it will often miss those. Our test shows where it fails: both knotweed photos it missed were taken in April and May, when the plant is a young shoot. A plant from a group it never saw in training (anything outside the look-alikes above) may get a random answer. Photos from far away, in bad light or blurry perform worse. Confidence scores are not probabilities you can trust: the model can be 98% sure and wrong. It called a bindweed (*Calystegia sepium*) knotweed with 98% confidence. Because iNaturalist users photograph "interesting" plants, the photos are not like the random snapshot a passer-by takes, so real-world accuracy will probably be lower than our test score. The test set has only 73 photos (26 knotweed), so one mistake more or less moves the score by about 1.4 percentage points, and the knotweed-found rate by about 4. A fair next test would use new photos we take ourselves.

**What could go wrong if it were used for real decisions.** A false "not knotweed" means a patch is not reported and keeps spreading, which is the expensive mistake. A false "knotweed" could make someone cut or poison a harmless native plant. Cutting real knotweed without the right method spreads it further, so the app must never replace an expert. It should only decide "worth reporting", and a trained person should check every report before anyone removes a plant. Location data in reports could also reveal where people live, so reports should share only the plant's location.

**What we already did to reduce these risks.** The app only says "likely knotweed" at 70% confidence or more; below that it says "maybe" and asks for a closer photo. Every answer carries a warning that it can be wrong and that an expert should check before anything is removed. The app gives no removal instructions, only "don't cut it, report it". We split the data by plant, so our test score is not inflated by the model seeing the same plant twice. We report how many knotweed photos it missed, not just overall accuracy. The app itself saves no photos or locations, but each photo is sent to Roboflow's servers to be classified, so users should not photograph people or house numbers. **Next steps** would be testing on our own spring photos and adding winter and spring images to the training data.
